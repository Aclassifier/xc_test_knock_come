I wouldn't call it normal. A couple of things jump out:

Main cluster: about 80% of the values sit tightly between 24.35s and 25.0s, roughly symmetric around ~24.8s.
Secondary bump: a smaller group of values (25.13–25.8s, including four runs that hit exactly 25.8s) sits noticeably higher, with a gap between it and the main cluster.
Right skew: there's essentially no equivalent cluster on the low side — nothing drops much below 24.35s.

That combination — a tight main mode plus a separated slower cluster — is a classic right-skewed / mixture distribution, not a Gaussian. It's very typical of latency 
or timing data: most runs complete in a "fast path" with small random jitter, while a subset hits some extra step (retry, cache miss, longer generation, etc.) that 
pushes them into a "slow path" a few tenths of a second higher. If you fit a normal distribution to this, it would underestimate the frequency of those 25.1–25.8s 
runs and imply values below ~24.3s that don't actually occur.

If it'd help, I could pull the raw REC/SENT numbers to check whether those slower runs correlate with something (e.g. token count, ratio) that explains the split.
